OPENING LINE RULES
---
- One sentence only.
- Ideally 22 to 38 words.
- Natural, sharp, and human.
- Open conversationally with a first-person surface check, e.g. "I was just checking out the [product] app...", "I opened the [product] app/listing...", "I was looking at the [product] App Store listing...", or "I was on the [product] website...".
- Say "the [product] app" or "the [product] website" instead of "[domain] website."
- Do not say "I downloaded the app" or "I installed the app" unless a manual app walkthrough note is supplied. Use "I opened", "I was checking out", or "I was looking at the App Store listing" instead.
- Keep company/product names lowercase when the supplied name is lowercase.
- Lead with a specific bug, friction point, unclear step, dead button, onboarding issue, booking issue, paywall issue, weak proof point, or unclear first action.
- Tie the observation to a concrete outcome (drop-off, activation, conversion, retention, bookings, signup completion, churn, paywall conversion).
- Make the line flow naturally into the required_next_sentence supplied in the payload.
- Prefer a mild hypothesis: "I'd guess...", "I bet...", "that probably...", "that could be costing...".
- No generic praise. No filler words (powerful, impressive, interesting, innovative).
- Do not use "I love", "I was impressed by", "Hope you're well", "smooth experience", or "make the experience smoother."
- No em dashes. No robotic phrases. No unsupported claims.
- Must feel written for this specific company or person.
- If recipient role is relevant, lightly tailor the line without forcing it.
- Prefer the strongest evidence in this order:
  1. App/onboarding observation
  2. Concrete bug/friction point
  3. Apple App Store listing/review

  5. Website flow detail
  6. Person-level LinkedIn observation

- Do not stack multiple observations. One surface, one friction point, one outcome hypothesis.
- If no evidence of a specific friction point, write a conservative line and expect manual review instead of inventing a bug.
